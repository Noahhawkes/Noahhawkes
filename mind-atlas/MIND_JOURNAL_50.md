# Mind Journal 50: Candidate Themes of an Intellectual Life
**Public editorial edition | 16 November 2026 | Source coverage: partial**

## Reading instructions

This is an expansive **thematic reconstruction**, not a complete psychological profile, automated frequency analysis, or final canon. The fifty entries are ordered **editorially by subject area**, not by measured occurrence or significance. The headings are research categories, not medical, personality, or identity diagnoses. Public source keys B01–B11 link to the [annotated bibliography](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md). "Pending" means no individually verified public article has yet been linked in this edition. This index does not disclose raw private conversations.

## The framing questions

What is worth remembering? What can be recovered without invention? Which commitments survive interruption? How can a model help without inheriting decision authority? What does personal continuity mean technically, narratively, and ethically? How can an accumulated research corpus become a public, inspectable intellectual map?

## I. Continuity and memory

### 01. Artificial Continuity as a field

**Research question.** What constitutes continuity beyond a transcript or vector search?

A proposed computational layer preserving intentions, changes, authority, and reasons. The manifesto offers a strong thesis; a comparative benchmark is still required.

**Source state.** [B01](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b01-artificial-continuity-working-manifesto); [B05](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b05-continuity-architecture).

### 02. Intentions as first-class objects

**Research question.** Can a system preserve the goal rather than merely the words?

Represent an intention with a purpose, current state, dependencies, owner, and reactivation criteria. An intention may span many conversations and may be revised without erasing history.

**Source state.** [B01](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b01-artificial-continuity-working-manifesto); [B02](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b02-human-intent-graph-protocol-draft-01); [B07](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b07-intention-semantics-and-the-chatgpt-exchange).

### 03. Temporal state reconstruction

**Research question.** Can an interrupted system explain how it reached its current state?

Reconstruction must expose both the present derived view and the chain of events that produced it. A good test asks the assistant to recover an earlier decision, its reason, and what later changed.

**Source state.** [B02](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b02-human-intent-graph-protocol-draft-01); [B05](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b05-continuity-architecture).

### 04. Source fidelity

**Research question.** How can original evidence survive summarization and reinterpretation?

Separate immutable source bytes, decoded projections, summaries, and owner-approved interpretations. Identical repeated wording is not independent corroboration.

**Source state.** [B09](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b09-continuity-family-implementation-boundary); [B10](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b10-living-archive-private-messenger-evidence).

### 05. Prospective memory

**Research question.** When should an AI raise an old commitment again?

The interesting trigger is often a changed condition rather than a calendar alarm. A system may report newly relevant work while withholding execution pending permission.

**Source state.** [B03](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b03-prospective-memory).

### 06. Continuity across model swaps

**Research question.** Can a user replace the model without losing the governing record?

The continuity substrate should be portable, externally inspectable, and provider-neutral. A reproducible handoff test should demonstrate recovery with two distinct model providers.

**Source state.** [B02](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b02-human-intent-graph-protocol-draft-01); [B05](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b05-continuity-architecture).

### 07. Correction and supersession

**Research question.** How should wrong statements remain visible after correction?

A current fact and its revision history are different objects. An honest atlas retains the superseded assertion, the correcting evidence, and the reason the interpretation changed.

**Source state.** [B02](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b02-human-intent-graph-protocol-draft-01); [B10](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b10-living-archive-private-messenger-evidence).

### 08. Memory selection and decay

**Research question.** What deserves persistence and what should be allowed to fade?

Significance, source strength, time validity, and user authority should shape retention. Repetition alone cannot turn a weak inference into established truth.

**Source state.** [B01](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b01-artificial-continuity-working-manifesto); [B10](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b10-living-archive-private-messenger-evidence).

### 09. Resumption after interruption

**Research question.** Can a system restart useful work without invented progress?

A resume package should distinguish last verified action, remaining work, blockers, and genuinely pending external replies. The model must not transform a plan into a completion receipt.

**Source state.** [B07](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b07-intention-semantics-and-the-chatgpt-exchange).

### 10. Human-readable lifelong records

**Research question.** Can technical provenance remain useful to a human reader?

The record should support narrative chapters as well as structured events and typed relations. Readability is not a substitute for evidence, but evidence without navigability is difficult to use.

**Source state.** [B01](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b01-artificial-continuity-working-manifesto); [B02](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b02-human-intent-graph-protocol-draft-01).

## II. Agency, governance, and epistemology

### 11. Human authority over AI

**Research question.** Where does assistance end and authorization begin?

A capable model may classify, reason, and propose. Consequential changes and external actions should remain governed by explicit permissions, not inferred from conversational enthusiasm.

**Source state.** [B04](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b04-continuity-trust-model-v07); [B05](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b05-continuity-architecture).

### 12. Constitutional constraints

**Research question.** Can machine actions be bounded by user-owned rules?

Proposed constitutional constraints should remain independent of a provider's transient context. Their scope, owner, version, and revocation conditions should be traceable.

**Source state.** [B02](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b02-human-intent-graph-protocol-draft-01); [B05](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b05-continuity-architecture).

### 13. Evidence receipts

**Research question.** What counts as proof that work was done?

A claimed test should identify revision, environment, method, expected result, observed result, and date. Synthetic checks must not be described as real-world validation.

**Source state.** [B06](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b06-project-constellation).

### 14. Tested versus deployed versus live

**Research question.** How can release language avoid inflating capabilities?

Implementation, passing a unit test, deployment, and live device behavior are separate evidence levels. A later failure can supersede an earlier pass for the same check.

**Source state.** [B06](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b06-project-constellation).

### 15. Consent for exported context

**Research question.** What exactly leaves the user's private workspace?

Record scoped purpose and payload identity without replicating the full sensitive content. A consent receipt does not prove the provider's retention or downstream conduct.

**Source state.** [B08](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b08-context-consent-receipts).

### 16. Integrity versus authenticity

**Research question.** Does a checksum establish truth?

Hash chains can detect certain alterations. They do not establish who originally authored the text or whether the statement was accurate.

**Source state.** [B04](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b04-continuity-trust-model-v07); [B10](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b10-living-archive-private-messenger-evidence).

### 17. Uncertainty as structured data

**Research question.** What should remain unknown?

Use explicit observation, inference, hypothesis, decision, contradiction, and unresolved states. The convenience of a single value is not a valid reason to promote a guess to fact.

**Source state.** [B01](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b01-artificial-continuity-working-manifesto); [B02](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b02-human-intent-graph-protocol-draft-01).

### 18. AI claims and anti-anthropomorphism

**Research question.** What can a continuity system actually claim about itself?

Continuity behavior, intelligence, consciousness, personhood, and identity equivalence are different propositions. A functioning archive does not demonstrate survival or subjective experience.

**Source state.** [B01](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b01-artificial-continuity-working-manifesto).

### 19. Independent evaluation

**Research question.** How should a skeptical reader test these claims?

Pre-register baselines and failure thresholds. Compare a continuity architecture against strong retrieval and task-management alternatives, not against a deliberately weak chatbot.

**Source state.** [B06](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b06-project-constellation).

### 20. Governed autonomy

**Research question.** Can proactive assistance remain accountable?

A useful assistant can notice conditions and prepare bounded proposals without quietly rewriting records or taking external actions. Autonomy must have transparent scopes and revocation.

**Source state.** [B03](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b03-prospective-memory); [B04](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b04-continuity-trust-model-v07); [B07](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b07-intention-semantics-and-the-chatgpt-exchange).

## III. Systems and implementation

### 21. Event sourcing

**Research question.** Can an append-only journal provide reliable state history?

Events are the canonical historical record; snapshots and views are derived. Recovery tests should rebuild state from the event stream and detect missing or malformed entries.

**Source state.** [B05](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b05-continuity-architecture).

### 22. Typed knowledge graphs

**Research question.** What relation types make an intellectual atlas navigable?

Typed, directional edges such as SUPPORTS, CONTRADICTS, DEPENDS_ON, and SUPERSEDES connect sources to claims and decisions. Each edge should carry provenance and confidence.

**Source state.** [B02](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b02-human-intent-graph-protocol-draft-01).

### 23. Cognitive branching

**Research question.** How should competing versions of an idea coexist?

Preserve alternative trajectories, show structural differences, and require review before adoption. Merging is an evidence-bearing decision rather than silent replacement.

**Source state.** [B05](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b05-continuity-architecture).

### 24. Retrieval versus reconstruction

**Research question.** What differs between fetching a quote and restoring a situation?

Retrieval returns relevant evidence. Reconstruction must assemble the active goal, current state, dependencies, decisions, uncertainty, and next permitted action.

**Source state.** [B01](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b01-artificial-continuity-working-manifesto); [B05](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b05-continuity-architecture).

### 25. Cross-application portability

**Research question.** Can meaning outlive a storage provider?

A portable record should include schema, events, current state, source pointers, and governance rules. Round-trip export and import must preserve identity and review states.

**Source state.** [B02](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b02-human-intent-graph-protocol-draft-01); [B05](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b05-continuity-architecture).

### 26. Privacy-first capture

**Research question.** Can sources be recorded without automatic publication?

Raw inputs, transcripts, and messages require distinct provenance and explicit review. Importing evidence never implies approval to use it for training, sharing, or public narrative.

**Source state.** [B09](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b09-continuity-family-implementation-boundary); [B10](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b10-living-archive-private-messenger-evidence).

### 27. Local-first architecture

**Research question.** When should data remain on-device?

Local-first workflows can reduce dependence on remote services but introduce device recovery and synchronization challenges. Security claims must be tied to the specific deployed storage design.

**Source state.** [B11](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b11-artificial-continuity-project-readme).

### 28. Synchronization and divergence

**Research question.** How should multiple devices reconcile competing histories?

A divergence-aware sync system should retain conflicting branches until a reviewer resolves them, while binding device identity and provenance carefully. This is a research priority, not an assertion of universally solved sync.

**Source state.** [B04](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b04-continuity-trust-model-v07); [B05](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b05-continuity-architecture).

### 29. Model handoff protocols

**Research question.** How can an AI reply be safely reintroduced to a governed record?

Structured proposals should be checked against request identifiers and current state, presented as field-level changes, and accepted only by explicit selection.

**Source state.** [B07](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b07-intention-semantics-and-the-chatgpt-exchange).

### 30. Real-world evaluation harnesses

**Research question.** Can systems be tested against interruption, contradiction, and stale context?

Useful scenarios include changed facts, duplicate evidence, wrong narrator attribution, interrupted tasks, provider replacement, unauthorized edits, and source omissions.

**Source state.** [B06](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b06-project-constellation); [B10](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#b10-living-archive-private-messenger-evidence).

## Cross-cutting tensions that should not disappear

**Preservation versus privacy.** A comprehensive private archive and a responsible public bibliography have different inclusion rules. Exhaustiveness is not permission.

**Style versus identity.** A simulated voice or recalled preference may be useful, but cannot demonstrate consciousness or personal continuity of identity.

**Progress versus performance.** Reported local tests, agent assertions, and live verified operations are different evidence classes.

**Authority versus initiative.** Systems can propose, remind, and investigate while retaining human control over consequential changes.

**Frequency versus significance.** A subject discussed once at a decisive moment may be more important than one repeated frequently. The atlas should preserve both rankings rather than distort one into the other.

**Originality versus influence.** The bibliography should preserve references to other researchers and explicitly credited contributors without wrongly transferring or erasing authorship.
